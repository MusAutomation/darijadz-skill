# Writing system: scripts, spelling, loanwords

Algerians write Darija in three ways. Pick one per text and stay consistent.

| Script | Looks like | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| **Mixed** (default) | `راني نخدم على le projet، نبعثلك le lien دروك` | social media, DMs, scripts, captions, most business |
| **Arabic letters only** | `راني نخدم على البروجي، نبعثلك الليان دروك` | the user asks; some teachers, parents and older readers |
| **Arabizi** | `rani nkhdem 3la le projet, nab3athlek le lien drok` | the user asks or writes to you in Arabizi (mirror them) |

## Mixed script: what goes in which letters

3. **Real code-switching → Latin letters.** These are words and phrases that stay French or English: `stop desk`, `livraison gratuite`, `la qualité`, `taille`, `pointure`, `promo`, `la dérivée`, `le TD`, `le mémoire`, `prompt`, `automation`, `workflow`, `deadline`, `devis`, `follow-up`, `bug`, `franchement`, `en tout cas`.
4. **Grey zone.** Some words go either way, such as promo / البرومو, story / ستوري, live / لايف. Either is fine, but stay consistent inside one text.
5. **Brand and product names** stay as written by the brand: `iPhone`, `Claude`, `TikTok`.

### Articles on Latin words
- With a French noun, keep the French article: `le projet`, `la livraison`, `les clients`.
- With English or technical words, use **الـ** and a space: `الـ automation`, `الـ agent`, `الـ TD`.
- Never glue ال onto Latin letters (`الautomation` is wrong).

### French verbs with Darija grammar
French verbs take Darija prefixes and endings, written **in Arabic letters**: نكوموندي (I order), يكونفيرمي (he confirms), بارطاجيو (share it, plural), نشارجي (I charge), بلوكاني (he blocked me), نفيكسيو موعد (we set a date), ما ريبونديليش (he didn't reply to me).
For tech audiences only, a hybrid like `نـfixi` or `يـrépondi` is acceptable, but Arabic letters are the default.

## Letters
- **ڨ** (or ق / ك) for the hard g sound: ڨاع، ڨال، ڨوڨل. Most people simply write ق or ك. Use ڨ only when the meaning would otherwise be ambiguous.
- **ڤ** for v in a few words (ڤيديو), though most people write فيديو. پ and چ are rare; write ب and تش (`تشيكي`).
- Hamza is often dropped in casual writing (انا، اللي، انشالله). That's fine, so don't force MSA hamzas.
- Use ة at the end of feminine words (مليحة، السومة), and ـو for "his / him" (تاعو، قالّو).
- Don't use tanween (جداً، شكراً): it marks MSA. `مثلا` is fine. <!-- lint:ignore -->
- Use Arabic punctuation inside Arabic text (، ؟) and Latin punctuation inside Latin segments.
- Use **Western digits** (0-9), as Algerians do. Don't use ٠١٢.

|---|---|---|

| ش | ch | chouf (شوف) |
| ج | dj or j | djit (جيت) |
| ث / ذ / ظ | th / dh / dh | thani (ثاني), dhar (ظهر) |
| ط / ص / ض | t / s / dh | tri9 (طريق), sa7a (صحة) |
| ء | 2 (rare) | t2ajjel (تأجل) |
| و / ي | ou, w / i, y | mouch, wach / bik, ya3tik |

Common spellings people recognise: `wach rak`, `rani labas`, `bezzaf`, `chwiya`, `ch7al`, `kayen`, `makanch`, `nchallah`, `sa7a`, `ya3tik sa7a`, `khouya`, `drok`, `ghodwa`, `mli7`.
French words stay French inside Arabizi: `la livraison gratuite`, `stop desk`, `le prix`.

## Full Arabic letters mode
Transliterate the French or English words: `ستوب ديسك`، `تاي M`، `الكواليتي`، `البروجي`، `الليان`. Keep brand names in Latin if the user prefers.
