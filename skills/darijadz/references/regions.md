# Regional flavours

Algeria is huge, and Darija sounds different in Oran, Algiers, Constantine and the south. **The default in this skill is the urban central Darija that every Algerian understands.** Use a regional flavour only when the user asks for it, writes that way themselves, or targets a specific city.

The counts are word frequencies in a 34M-word corpus of Algerian speech, to show how widespread a form is. Regional assignments are tendencies, not borders.

<!-- lint:off -->
## West (Oran, Tlemcen, Mostaganem, Sidi Bel Abbès, Mascara, Relizane)
- **وشنو** (what) is very common (≈20k), alongside واش.
- Question words ending in -اه: **علاه** (why, 16k), **كيفاه** (how, 13k), **وقتاه** (when).
- **غادي** marks the future (≈21k): `غادي نروح` (I'm going to go). The default راح is understood everywhere.
- نبغي / بغيت (to want, to love): `نبغيك` (I love you). In the centre and east it's نحب.
- إلا means "if": `إلا جيت عيطلي`.
- قاع / ڨاع is said with a g.
- The far west, near the border, shares a few words with Morocco (جوج، مزيان), but ديال and دابا remain Moroccan.

## Centre (Algiers, Blida, Boumerdès, Tipaza, Médéa)
- واش / علاش / كيفاش / وقتاش, plus دروك or درك.
- Heavy French code-switching in the cities, plus many French loans in Arabic letters.
- Typical words: زعما، يا خو، الصوارد (money), بالاك، شحال، الدراري.
- This is the reference variety for national media, ads and social media.

## East (Constantine, Annaba, Sétif, Batna, Jijel, Skikda, Guelma)
- **قداه** (how much) is used as well as شحال.
- In many eastern areas, **نتي** means "you" for both a man and a woman, and the verb doesn't change for gender (`نتي تدير`).
- Near the Tunisian border (Annaba, El Tarf, Souk Ahras, Tébessa) some Tunisian-like words appear, such as توّا (now) and باهي (good, OK). Don't use them by default.
- Some speakers say ڨ (g) for ق: `ڨال` (he said).

## South (Ghardaïa, Ouargla, Biskra, Béchar, Adrar, Tamanrasset, El Oued)
- A more Bedouin sound: ق is pronounced g (ڨال، ڨاع), and there are older Arabic words.
- أتاي (tea) is everywhere. Hospitality formulas matter.
- Tamazight languages (Mozabite, Tuareg) and Arabic coexist.

## Kabylie and Amazigh regions (Tizi Ouzou, Béjaïa, Bouira, the Aurès, the Mzab)
- For many people Kabyle, Chaoui or another Tamazight variety is the first language, and Darija and French come next. Don't assume Arabic is anyone's mother tongue.
- Darija there mixes freely with Kabyle words and lots of French.
- Respectful mentions: `يناير` (Yennayer, 12 January), `تانميرت` (thank you, in Kabyle).

## Writing for a regional audience
1. Keep the grammar of the default variety.
2. Swap 3 to 5 signature words (وشنو / علاه / غادي for Oran, قداه for Constantine…). <!-- lint:ignore -->
3. Add a local reference (a neighbourhood, a dish, a team) **only if the user provides it**. Don't invent local facts.
4. If the user corrects a word, use their word for the rest of the conversation.
